Frame (c) · route /settings/access

Remove member

Removing someone revokes their access to the whole organization. As with demotion, two cases are refused: an organization can never be left with no admin, and never with no owner.

(c1) Ordinary removal — confirm

Remove rhea@northwind.example?

They lose access to Northwind immediately

Their organization_memberships row is deleted and the matching Permit role grant is revoked; active sessions for this org end. You can invite them again later, but their role won't be restored automatically.
(c2) Last admin — refused (409)

Remove grace@northwind.example?

Northwind needs at least one admin

Grace is the only admin. Promote someone else to admin first, then remove Grace.
(c3) Last owner — refused (409)

Remove ada@northwind.example?

Northwind needs at least one owner

Ada is the only owner. Transfer ownership first by promoting someone to owner, then remove Ada. An organization is never left ownerless.

Contract. DELETE /v1/security/tenants/{tenantId}/members/{userId} (idempotent → 204). Removing an already-absent member still returns 204, so the UI treats a 204 and a 404-then-refresh identically: the row is gone. Success revokes the member's Permit role grant for this org in the same owned write.

⚠ Both refusals are commissioned by this frame. (c2) 409 LAST_ADMIN and (c3) 409 LAST_OWNER — today the delete would succeed and leave the org unadministrable or ownerless. Approving this commissions both server guards plus the client pre-checks that disable the button. The disabled button is an affordance, not the guard — the server must refuse independently.

403 is not this. A caller without the organization:manage-members permission gets a 403 FORBIDDEN (frame (e)) — an authorization denial shown in place, never a sign-in redirect. The last-admin / last-owner 409s are different: the caller is allowed, but the action is refused to protect the org.
